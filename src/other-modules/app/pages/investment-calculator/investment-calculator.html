Learn More About These Numbers

Why index funds?

Most individual investors can't reliably beat the market, and fees compound against you over decades. A fund that tracks the S&P 500 gives you ownership in roughly 500 of the largest U.S. companies for a fraction of a percent in annual fees — the "buy the whole market and hold" approach behind the Bogleheads investing philosophy.

Sources: SEC Investor.gov — Index Funds, Bogleheads Wiki — Three-Fund Portfolio

Why 10% by default?

Since 1926, the S&P 500 has returned about 10% a year on average before inflation (roughly 6.5–7% after inflation). Any single year can swing far above or below that — this is a very long-run average, not a guarantee.

Source: officialdata.org — S&P 500 Returns Since 1926

Why a ~3.5% withdrawal rate instead of the famous 4%?

The "4% rule" comes from William Bengen's 1994 research and the 1998 Trinity Study, both of which tested a 30-year retirement — not an indefinite one. If you want a portfolio to fund withdrawals forever without shrinking, you generally need to withdraw a bit less. Morningstar's own updated retirement-income research has put a sustainable starting rate as low as 3.3% in some years (3.9% in its most recent estimate), depending on forward-looking return and inflation assumptions. At roughly 3.2–3.5%, every $1,000,000 invested supports about $32,000–$35,000 a year indefinitely — this calculator defaults to 3.5%.

Sources: Wikipedia — 4% Rule, Morningstar — Reevaluating the 4% Withdrawal Rule

What does this mean for retirement?

The "25x rule" used by the FIRE (Financial Independence, Retire Early) community is just the 4% rule in reverse: save 25 times your annual expenses, and a 4% withdrawal should sustain you indefinitely. In practice, many people consider something in the $1M–$3M range enough to retire comfortably depending on their expenses and location, with $3M+ generally affording a very comfortable lifestyle almost anywhere. Your actual number depends entirely on your own spending, not a fixed dollar amount.

Source: Wikipedia — FIRE Movement
